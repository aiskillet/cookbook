# Cold Outreach

A cold email earns a reply when it's short, obviously personalized, and about *their* problem with one easy ask. Everything else gets deleted in three seconds.

## When to Activate
- Cold sales, fundraising, partnership, or hiring outreach
- Asking a stranger for a meeting, intro, or feedback

## The anatomy
1. **Subject:** short, specific, human. Lowercase, 2–5 words. No "Quick question re: synergy."
2. **First line = them, not you.** A specific, real reason you're writing to *them* (something they built/said/shipped). Generic = deleted.
3. **The point, fast:** who you are in half a sentence, and why it's relevant to them.
4. **One clear, low-friction ask:** "Worth a 15-min call next week?" or "Can I send a 2-line summary?" Not five asks.
5. **Make replying trivial** — a yes/no or a pick-a-time. Short enough to read on a phone (≤5 sentences).

## Principles
- **Personalize the opener for real** — one genuine, specific detail beats a mail-merge {{first_name}}.
- **Lead with their benefit / their world**, not your feature list.
- **Brevity is respect.** If it looks long, it won't be read.
- **One CTA.** Multiple asks = no action.
- **Follow up 2–3 times**, spaced, adding a new angle each time — most replies come from follow-ups. Then stop gracefully.
- **Credibility in one line** — a relevant proof point (customer, traction, mutual connection), not a resume.

## Avoid
- "Just checking in" with no new value.
- Walls of text / feature dumps.
- Fake familiarity or flattery.
- Asking for a lot before giving anything.

## Checklist
- [ ] Specific, human subject
- [ ] Opener is about them (real detail)
- [ ] ≤5 sentences; readable on a phone
- [ ] Exactly one easy ask
- [ ] One credibility line; a planned, value-adding follow-up
