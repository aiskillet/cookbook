# Positioning

Positioning is the context you set so people instantly "get" what you are and why it's for them. Get it right and messaging, landing pages, and sales all become easy. Get it wrong and no amount of copywriting saves you.

## When to Activate
- Writing a value proposition, tagline, or landing page
- Pitching a product or preparing a deck
- A product that people "don't get" quickly
- Choosing the category you compete in

## The five components (April Dunford's frame)
1. **Competitive alternatives** — what would people use if you didn't exist? (Often a spreadsheet or "nothing," not a competitor.)
2. **Unique attributes** — what you have that alternatives don't.
3. **Value** — what those attributes let the customer *do* or achieve (benefit, not feature).
4. **Best-fit customers** — who cares most about that value.
5. **Market category** — the frame that makes your value obvious. The category sets expectations.

## How to use it
- **Lead with the value, framed for the best-fit customer.** "For X who struggle with Y, we do Z" beats a feature list.
- **Pick the category deliberately** — it primes what people compare you to. The wrong category makes your strengths look irrelevant.
- **Differentiate on what you uniquely enable**, not adjectives ("fast", "easy" — everyone says that).
- **Name the alternative honestly** and show why you're better *for the best-fit customer*.

## Test it
Can a stranger, after one sentence, say what you are and whether it's for them? If not, the positioning — not the copy — is the problem.

## Checklist
- [ ] Named the real alternative (often "nothing"/spreadsheet)
- [ ] Unique attributes → concrete value
- [ ] Best-fit customer identified
- [ ] Category chosen to make value obvious
- [ ] Value leads the message, not features
