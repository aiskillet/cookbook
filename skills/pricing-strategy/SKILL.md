# Pricing Strategy

Pricing is the highest-leverage growth lever and the least experimented-with. Price on the **value you create**, align the model to how that value scales, and treat pricing as something you test, not set once.

## When to Activate
- Setting initial pricing or revising it
- Designing tiers / packaging
- Writing a pricing page or a pricing section of a deck

## Start from value, not cost
- **Cost-plus is a trap.** Anchor to the value/ROI the customer gets and what alternatives cost them.
- **Find willingness to pay** — ask (Van Westendorp: "at what price is it too expensive / a bargain?"), look at what they pay for alternatives, and test real prices.
- **Segment** — different customers get different value; capture it with tiers, not one price.

## Pick a model that scales with value
- **Per-seat** (value grows with users), **usage-based** (grows with consumption), **flat/tiered** (simple), or a **hybrid** (platform fee + usage). Choose the metric that tracks the customer's value and is easy to understand.
- **Good–better–best tiers:** 3 tiers, anchor with the high one, make the middle the obvious choice. Gate on value metrics, not arbitrary limits.

## Practical rules
- **Charge more than feels comfortable** — most founders underprice. You can discount down; raising is harder.
- **Simple and legible** beats clever — if buyers can't predict their bill, they hesitate.
- **Annual plans** (discounted) improve cash + retention.
- **Grandfather existing customers** on increases to keep trust.
- **Iterate** — pricing is a series of experiments; measure conversion and expansion, adjust.

## Checklist
- [ ] Priced on value/ROI, not cost
- [ ] Willingness-to-pay evidence gathered
- [ ] Model tracks the customer's value metric
- [ ] 3 legible tiers; middle is the obvious pick
- [ ] Not underpriced; annual option; plan to test & iterate
