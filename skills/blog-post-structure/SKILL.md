# Blog Post Structure

Most posts lose the reader in the first 3 sentences. Structure for the skimmer: earn the click with a hook, deliver value fast, and make it scannable.

## When to Activate
- Writing a technical, product, or founder blog post
- A draft that "drags" or buries its point
- Turning notes/a talk into a post

## The shape
1. **Hook (first 2–3 lines):** a specific problem, surprising claim, or the payoff. No throat-clearing ("In today's fast-paced world…"). Make them want line 2.
2. **The promise:** what they'll get / be able to do by the end. Set the frame.
3. **Body:** one idea per section, descriptive subheads, short paragraphs (2–4 lines), examples/code/visuals. Front-load value — don't save the good stuff for the end.
4. **One core takeaway:** a post should leave the reader with *one* thing. If you have five, write five posts.
5. **Close + CTA:** recap the takeaway, one clear next step (try it, read next, subscribe).

## Principles
- **Write for the skimmer first.** Subheads should tell the story on their own.
- **Concrete > abstract.** Real examples, numbers, and specifics beat generalities.
- **Cut ruthlessly.** Delete the first paragraph (it's usually warm-up) and any sentence that doesn't serve the takeaway.
- **Show your reasoning / a story** — narrative and specificity are what get shared.
- **Title + hook do 80% of the work** — spend real time on them.

## Checklist
- [ ] Hook lands in the first 2–3 lines (no warm-up)
- [ ] Clear promise of what the reader gets
- [ ] Scannable: subheads, short paragraphs, examples
- [ ] Exactly one core takeaway
- [ ] Strong title; close with one CTA
