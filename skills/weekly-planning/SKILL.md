# Weekly Planning

A good weekly plan trades a long to-do list for a short list of outcomes that actually move things. Plan around a handful of priorities, not a backlog.

## When to Activate
- Starting a week or sprint
- Overwhelmed / everything feels urgent
- Reviewing whether last week's effort mattered

## The routine
1. **Review last week** — what shipped, what slipped, what did I learn? Carry over honestly (don't just roll a growing pile).
2. **Pick 3 priorities** — the outcomes that would make the week a win. If everything is a priority, nothing is.
3. **Time-block them first** — put the big-rock work on the calendar before meetings fill it. Unscheduled priorities don't happen.
4. **Batch the small stuff** — group shallow tasks (email, reviews) into a block or two; don't let them fragment deep-work time.
5. **Leave slack** — ~20% unplanned for the inevitable surprises. A 100%-booked week breaks on Tuesday.

## Principles
- **Outcomes over tasks.** "Ship the export feature" beats "work on export."
- **Protect one deep-work block/day** — no meetings, no Slack.
- **Say no on purpose** — every yes is a no to a priority. Decline or defer explicitly.
- **Make it visible** — one place you actually look at daily.

## Friday close-out
Spend 15 min: what got done, what's next, inbox/notes to zero. You'll start Monday clear instead of reconstructing.

## Checklist
- [ ] Reviewed last week honestly
- [ ] 3 priorities named as outcomes
- [ ] Priorities time-blocked before meetings
- [ ] Small tasks batched; one deep block/day protected
- [ ] ~20% slack left; plan is somewhere visible
