# Literature Review

A literature review isn't a pile of summaries — it's a synthesis that shows what's known, what's contested, and where the gap is. Organize by *idea*, not by paper.

## When to Activate
- Researching a topic in depth (technical, academic, or market)
- Writing a background / related-work section
- Scoping prior art before building or claiming novelty

## Process
1. **Define the question** and scope (what's in, what's out) before searching — prevents an endless pile.
2. **Search broadly, then snowball:** start with recent surveys/reviews, then follow their citations backward and forward (who cited this?). Surveys are the fastest map.
3. **Screen for relevance and quality** — skim abstract/intro/conclusion first; read fully only what survives.
4. **Extract into a matrix** — one row per source: question, method, key finding, limitation. This is what lets you synthesize instead of summarize.
5. **Synthesize by theme** — group findings into themes/debates; note where sources agree, disagree, and why.
6. **Name the gap** — what's unanswered or weakly supported. That's the point of the whole exercise.

## Principles
- **Organize by concept, not by paper.** "Approaches to X" beats "Paper 1 says…, Paper 2 says…".
- **Prioritize primary sources** and recent authoritative work; note publication date (fields move fast).
- **Track everything** — citation, link, and your one-line takeaway — as you go; reconstructing later is painful.
- **Be critical, not just descriptive** — evaluate methods and evidence, don't just report claims.
- **Distinguish consensus from a single loud paper.**

## Output
A synthesis organized by theme, each with cited support, a table of key sources, and an explicit statement of the gap / open questions.

## Checklist
- [ ] Scoped question defined before searching
- [ ] Started from surveys; snowballed citations both directions
- [ ] Extraction matrix (method/finding/limitation) per source
- [ ] Synthesized by theme; agreement vs. disagreement noted
- [ ] Gap/open questions stated; every claim cited
