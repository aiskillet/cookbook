# Market Sizing

A credible market size is a *defensible estimate*, not a big number from a report. Do it two ways — top-down and bottom-up — and make them agree; that's what earns trust.

## When to Activate
- Sizing an opportunity for a plan, deck, or business case
- Sanity-checking whether a market is big enough
- Answering "how big can this get?"

## TAM / SAM / SOM
- **TAM** (Total Addressable Market) — everyone who could theoretically buy. The ceiling.
- **SAM** (Serviceable Addressable) — the segment you can actually serve (geography, segment, product fit).
- **SOM** (Serviceable Obtainable) — what you can realistically capture in a few years. The one investors actually weigh.

## Do it both ways (and reconcile)
- **Top-down:** start from a big published number, narrow by segment %. Fast, but soft — analyst reports are often optimistic. Use for orientation, cite sources.
- **Bottom-up (preferred):** `# of potential customers × price × frequency`. Build from real units. Far more defensible because every input is inspectable.
- **Reconcile them.** If top-down and bottom-up are 100× apart, an assumption is wrong — find it. Agreement is the credibility signal.

## Principles
- **Show your assumptions and math** — a number without the derivation is worthless. Make each input checkable.
- **Be conservative and cite** every input; flag the ones you're least sure of.
- **Value = customers × ACV**, not "1% of a huge market" (the classic hand-wave that convinces no one).
- **Segment** — different segments have different sizes and willingness to pay.
- **Sensitivity:** show the estimate under conservative / base / optimistic assumptions.

## Checklist
- [ ] TAM / SAM / SOM defined, not just one number
- [ ] Bottom-up built from customers × price × frequency
- [ ] Top-down cross-check; reconciled if they diverge
- [ ] Every assumption stated, sourced, and inspectable
- [ ] Conservative/base/optimistic range shown
