# Survey Design

A survey is only as good as its questions. Leading or vague questions produce confident-looking garbage. Design for the decision you'll make with the data.

## When to Activate
- Writing a survey, feedback form, NPS, or questionnaire
- Gathering quantitative signal from users/customers
- A survey that got low response or unusable answers

## Start from the decision
Ask: *what decision will this data drive?* Every question must serve it. Cut questions that are "nice to know" — they lower completion and dilute signal.

## Write neutral questions
- **No leading/loaded wording.** "How much did you love the new feature?" presumes love. Ask "How would you rate the new feature?" with a balanced scale.
- **One thing per question** — avoid double-barreled ("Was it fast and easy?" — which?).
- **Plain, specific language;** define terms; avoid jargon and absolutes ("always/never").
- **Balanced options** — equal positive/negative points; include a neutral midpoint and an "N/A"/"don't know" where honest.

## Choose the right response type
- **Likert (1–5/1–7)** for attitudes; label the ends.
- **Multiple choice** with mutually exclusive, exhaustive options (+ "Other").
- **Open-ended** sparingly — rich but hard to analyze; use for the "why".
- **NPS** (0–10 "how likely to recommend") for a standard benchmark.

## Structure & sampling
- **Short.** Every extra question drops completion. Put easy questions first, sensitive/demographic last.
- **Logical flow;** group related items; use skip logic to avoid irrelevant questions.
- **Sample matters** — who you ask and how you recruit drives bias more than wording. Beware self-selection.
- **Pilot it** on a few people first; fix confusing items before sending.

## Checklist
- [ ] Every question serves the decision; no filler
- [ ] Questions neutral, single-barreled, specific
- [ ] Scales balanced with midpoint / N-A
- [ ] Short; sensitive items last; skip logic used
- [ ] Piloted; sampling/bias considered
