# User Interviews

The goal is to learn what's true, not to hear that your idea is great. People are polite and bad at predicting their own behavior — so ask about what they've actually *done*, never what they *would* do.

## When to Activate
- Customer discovery / validating a problem
- Before building, to test an assumption
- Understanding why users churn or don't convert

## The golden rules (The Mom Test)
- **Ask about their life and past behavior, not your idea.** "Walk me through the last time you dealt with X" beats "Would you use a tool that…?"
- **Never pitch.** The moment you describe your solution, you get politeness, not truth.
- **Dig into specifics and emotion.** "What did you do next? How much did that cost you? How did you feel?"
- **Look for evidence, not compliments.** Real signals: they've spent money/time on it, have a workaround, tried other tools. "That's cool" is worthless.

## How to run one
1. **One clear learning goal** per interview — the riskiest assumption.
2. **Open-ended questions**, then shut up and listen (aim 80% them talking).
3. **Chase stories** — "tell me about the last time…"; follow the emotion and the effort.
4. **Ask what they've tried** and what it cost — hierarchy of needs made real.
5. **End with intros/next steps** — "who else should I talk to?"

## Avoid
- Leading questions ("don't you hate when…?").
- Hypotheticals and feature-voting.
- Talking more than the interviewee.
- Counting compliments as validation.

## After
Write up verbatim quotes + observed behaviors within an hour (memory decays). Look across interviews for patterns; update your riskiest-assumption list.

## Checklist
- [ ] One learning goal; questions about past behavior
- [ ] Didn't pitch; they talked ~80%
- [ ] Chased specifics: actions, cost, emotion
- [ ] Captured what they've already tried/paid for
- [ ] Notes written up immediately; patterns extracted
