# Meeting Notes

Notes exist to make the meeting count afterward. Capture **decisions and actions**, not a play-by-play. If it doesn't change what someone does, it probably doesn't belong.

## When to Activate
- Taking notes during a meeting
- Summarizing a call/transcript
- Writing a recap to send afterward

## The format that works
```
## <Topic> — <date>
Attendees: …
Decisions:
  - <decision> (why, in a few words)
Action items:
  - [ ] <task> — @owner — due <date>
Open questions:
  - <question> — @who will resolve
Notes:
  - <key context worth remembering>
```

## Principles
- **Decisions + owners + due dates are non-negotiable.** Every action has a single owner and a date; "we should" with no owner is a wish, not an action.
- **Summarize, don't transcribe.** Capture the conclusion and the *why*, skip the debate.
- **Write for someone who wasn't there.** Enough context to act without asking.
- **Real-time > from memory.** Capture decisions as they happen; memory decays fast.
- **Send the recap same day**, with actions surfaced at the top so people see their tasks.

## Anti-patterns
- A wall of transcript nobody rereads.
- Action items with no owner or date.
- Burying decisions inside paragraphs.

## Checklist
- [ ] Decisions captured with a one-line why
- [ ] Every action has an owner + due date
- [ ] Open questions assigned to someone
- [ ] Readable by an absent teammate
- [ ] Recap sent promptly, actions on top
