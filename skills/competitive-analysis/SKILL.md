# Competitive Analysis

The goal isn't a feature grid — it's finding the **gap you can own**. Be honest about competitors' strengths; wishful analysis leads to bad strategy.

## When to Activate
- Entering or sizing a market
- Planning product direction
- Writing a strategy or investor doc
- Deciding how to differentiate

## Do it in this order
1. **Define the frame** — who actually competes for your customer's problem, including the non-obvious alternative ("do nothing", a spreadsheet, an internal tool).
2. **Gather evidence, not impressions** — their site, pricing, docs, changelog, reviews (G2/Reddit/HN), job posts (reveal roadmap), support forums (reveal pain).
3. **Compare on what matters to the customer** — jobs-to-be-done, not just features. Include pricing model, target segment, positioning, and moat.
4. **Find the gap** — an underserved segment, an unmet job, a pricing/UX opening. That's your wedge.

## Principles
- **Steelman competitors.** Assume they're smart; understand *why* they made their choices.
- **Read the reviews' 3-star ones** — that's where real gaps hide.
- **Watch changelogs + job posts** for where they're heading, not just where they are.
- **Conclusion = a decision**, not a report: "we win by owning segment X with capability Y."

## Checklist
- [ ] Frame includes non-obvious alternatives
- [ ] Claims backed by evidence (links), not vibes
- [ ] Compared on customer jobs, not feature counts
- [ ] Competitors steelmanned
- [ ] Ends in a positioning decision / wedge
