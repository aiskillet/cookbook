# OKR Drafting

OKRs align a team on a few outcomes that matter. The common failure is writing a task list disguised as goals. Objectives inspire; Key Results measure *results*, not activity.

## When to Activate
- Planning a quarter/half; setting company or team goals
- Aligning multiple teams on shared priorities
- Fixing goals that are vague, a laundry list, or just a to-do list

## The shape
- **Objective** — qualitative, memorable, ambitious. *Where do we want to be?* (e.g., "Become the default tool for indie devs.")
- **Key Results (2–4)** — measurable outcomes that prove the Objective. *How do we know we got there?* Each has a number and a target.

```
Objective: Make onboarding effortless
  KR1: Activation rate 40% → 65%
  KR2: Time-to-first-value 20 min → under 5
  KR3: Week-1 retention 30% → 45%
```

## Rules that make them work
- **Measure outcomes, not output.** "Ship the new onboarding" is a task; "activation 40%→65%" is a result. If you can complete a KR without moving the business, it's wrong.
- **Few and focused.** 1 Objective, 2–4 KRs per team. More = no priorities.
- **Ambitious but not impossible** — stretch goals; ~70% attainment is often "good." If you always hit 100%, you're sandbagging.
- **Separate from compensation.** Tie bonuses to OKRs and people set safe, gameable targets.
- **Owned and visible** — each KR has an owner; review progress regularly (weekly/monthly), not just at quarter-end.
- **Cascade by alignment, not dictation** — teams draft KRs that ladder up to company Objectives; top-down + bottom-up.

## Anti-patterns
- KRs that are a task list ("launch X, hire Y").
- 7 objectives and 20 KRs (no focus).
- Metrics nobody can actually measure.

## Checklist
- [ ] Objective is qualitative, inspiring, memorable
- [ ] 2–4 KRs, each a measurable *outcome* with a target
- [ ] Ambitious (stretch), not sandbagged
- [ ] Owners assigned; reviewed on a cadence
- [ ] Decoupled from comp; ladders up to company goals
